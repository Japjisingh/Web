01 · What we will build

Four products in one app

Students learn the theory, see it in 3D, rehearse it in a sim, and prove it in exam-style tests. Each part feeds the others: a wrong exam answer links back to the lesson and the 3D scene that explains it.

Learn

3D Ground School

Every theory subject, taught one idea at a time. Each lesson has a question, the key fact, a 3D or interactive scene, a memory hook and a quick check.

Practise

Sim Training Guide

Briefings, flows, checklists and flight profiles for simulator sessions. It includes a browser cockpit trainer and scenario cards you fly in MSFS, X-Plane or your school's simulator.

Test

Exam Engine

A question bank tagged to each authority's syllabus, timed mock exams in each exam's format, spaced repetition and a map of your weak topics.

Run

Platform

Accounts, progress synced across phone, tablet and laptop, offline lessons, payments, a content editor for us, and an instructor dashboard for flying schools.

How every lesson works

  1. A question as the title"Why does the VOR needle ignore your heading?" Students learn by answering something.
  2. The key factOne or two sentences: the thing you must know for the exam.
  3. See itA 3D scene, animation, real photo or live tool you can move and test.
  4. Memory hookA short phrase that makes it stick, such as "High to low, look out below".
  5. CheckOne or two exam-style questions, with the answer explained.
  6. In one minuteA recap card for each chapter, used later in spaced-repetition review.

Tagged to learning objectives

Every lesson and question carries the syllabus reference for each authority. When you pick "EASA ATPL", you see exactly the lessons for 061 General Navigation, in that order.

Regional packs

Law, phraseology and climatology change by country. Each authority gets its own pack: Indian Aircraft Rules and CARs, EASA Part-FCL and SERA, the UK ANO and CAP 413, and US 14 CFR and AIM.

Units and conventions

Switch between hPa and inHg, metres and feet, ICAO and FAA chart symbols, and "aerodrome" or "airport". Exam timers and pass marks follow each authority's rules.

FAA knowledge tests covered: Private (PAR), Instrument (IRA), Commercial (CAX) and ATP Multi-engine (ATM). Before writing each track, we check every mapping against the authority's current published syllabus.

04 · 3D prototypes

Eight scenes you can fly right now

These run live in your browser: drag to turn, scroll or pinch to zoom, and use the controls on the right. They use the same 3D techniques the finished app will use.

Renderer

three.js r186 on WebGL 2, which works in every current browser. The app will move to three.js's WebGPU renderer, which falls back to WebGL 2 automatically. WebGPU now ships in Chrome, Edge and Safari 26 on iPhone, iPad and Mac.

Lighting

Physically based materials and a physical sky model, which also lights the scene's reflections. Soft shadows and film-style tone mapping.

Effects

Glow on lights (bloom), multisample anti-aliasing, a raymarched volumetric cloud, NASA Earth imagery and real aerofoil airflow.

Speed

Each scene starts only when you scroll near it and pauses when off screen. Resolution adjusts itself on slower phones and tablets.

  • Our own flight physics, tuned to published C172S figures, flying in wind, gusts and turbulence

Later

  • Works on tablets and laptops; phones are too small to fly well
  • No manufacturer logos or branding

06 · Jet procedure trainer

Airliner flows, practised before the simulator

A virtual procedure trainer with an A320-class flight deck. Work the overhead panel, pedestal and displays in the right order, from a cold and dark cockpit to both engines running, with the systems responding live. Built for cadets preparing for airline sim assessments and type ratings.

Sim Training Guide · JetsA320-class layout · generic type

Cockpit preparation and engine start

Choose Guided, Practice or Memory mode, then work through the flow. Tap or click switches, knobs and levers in the flight deck; the displays and lights respond as the systems come alive.

Drag to look round · tap a control to use it

What it trains

  • Where every switch is, and what it does
  • Flow order and muscle memory
  • Reading the system displays as things come online
  • Handling a fault during start

Generic by design

The aircraft is a fictional twin-jet with an A320-class layout. Flows and messages are written by us from general knowledge. They are not Airbus or any airline's procedures; always follow your operator's manuals.

Next

  • More system faults: electrical, hydraulic, bleed
  • Flight management set-up
  • Licensed operator procedures through a school or airline partner

07 · Companion mode

Train in X-Plane 12, scored by Glidepath

For the most realistic flying, students use a desktop simulator. Glidepath connects to X-Plane 12 on the same computer, reads the flight live, scores the exercise, lets an instructor trigger failures, and produces the debrief. Below is the companion running on a recorded demo flight; the same screen works live with X-Plane.

How it connects

X-Plane 12.1.1 and later has a built-in web interface on your computer (port 8086). A small Glidepath connector reads flight data from it and passes it to the companion page in your browser.

What you need

  • X-Plane 12.1.1 or later, with incoming connections allowed in Settings → Network
  • Node.js 22 or later to run the connector
  • Any browser on the same computer, or a tablet on the same home network

MSFS 2024

MSFS has no built-in web interface. It needs a small Windows helper using Microsoft's SimConnect SDK. The companion screen stays the same; only the connector changes.

What we will never use

  • Questions remembered from real exams, or "leaked" question banks
  • Text, diagrams or questions copied from other courses, apps or books, including only70.com
  • Airline SOPs, FCOMs or company manuals
  • Charts and plates reproduced from Jeppesen, Lido or national AIPs without permission

How a chapter gets written

  • Map the learning objectives for each authority
  • Draft the lessons and questions from the sources, with a reference for each fact
  • Build the 3D scenes and interactive pieces
  • An instructor reviews it, then you test it as a student
  • Recheck every AIRAC cycle, so rule changes are picked up

10 · Making money

Free to start, paid to pass

Use it yourself first, then open it to other students. The free tier brings people in; the exam engine and full chapters are what students pay for. Set prices after a beta. For reference, only70 advertises plans from ₹99.

Free

Taster

  • First chapters of every subject
  • 10 practice questions a day
  • Two 3D scenes per subject
Paid

  • One subject, all lessons and 3D
  • Full question bank and mock exams
  • 3, 6 or 12 months
Paid

All subjects

  • Every subject for your licence
  • Sim training guide
  • Study plan to your exam date
Business

Flying schools

  • Per-student seats
  • Instructor dashboard and assignments
  • School branding option

Payments

Razorpay in India (UPI, cards, net banking) and Stripe for international cards. Each country sees its own prices.

Legal basics

An independent study aid that is not approved by or affiliated with DGCA, EASA, the UK CAA, the FAA or ICAO. We also need terms, a refund policy, and privacy under India's DPDP Act and GDPR.

Growth

Instagram reels of the 3D scenes (as only70 does), free tools such as a METAR decoder and wind-triangle calculator, and partnerships with flying schools.

11 · Roadmap

Build order

Start with the parts nobody else has, prove they work for you, then widen to more authorities.

  1. Blueprint and prototypes This page

    • Plan, syllabus map, data sources, screen mockups, eight 3D prototypes
    • You choose the name, launch order and first subject

  2. Foundation

    • App shell, sign-in, licence picker, progress tracking
    • Lesson engine (question, fact, scene, hook, check) and 3D scene component
    • Exam engine core: question bank, practice mode, timed mocks

  3. First full subject: DGCA Air Navigation

    • General Navigation, Radio Navigation and Flight Planning chapters
    • All Navigation 3D scenes; question bank of around 1,000 questions
    • You study with it; an instructor reviews it

  4. Sim Training Guide v1

    • C172-class browser simulator: cockpit, scenarios, instructor failures and debrief
    • Flows and checklists linked to the sim; scenario cards for MSFS and X-Plane
    • Test JSBSim (open-source flight model) for more realistic handling

  5. Rest of DGCA CPL and launch

    • Technical General, Technical Specific, RTR(A), then Met and Air Regs
    • Payments, beta with student pilots, then public launch

  6. EASA and UK CAA ATPL

    • Map shared chapters to the 13 exam subjects; add EASA and UK law packs
    • Add the Mass & Balance, Performance and Ops depth that ATPL needs

  7. FAA and schools

    • US law pack, FAA question style, inHg and FAA charts
    • Instructor dashboard and school seats
